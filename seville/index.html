The place

Seville by night

Seville is the capital of Andalusia, in the south of Spain, on the banks of the Guadalquivir river. Its days are hot and bright, but its nights are something else: warm, slow and full of music.

Across the river lies Triana, the old neighbourhood of potters, sailors and flamenco singers, where many say the soul of flamenco was born. In the old town, narrow streets open onto patios with fountains, blue-and-white tiles and pots of geraniums.

The city is full of orange trees. In spring their blossom, the azahar, perfumes every street. The palace of the Alcázar has gardens of fountains, palms and roses, and the Giralda bell tower watches over it all.

The music on this channel is flamenco played softly: a nylon-string guitar, gentle rasgueado strums, quiet cajón and palmas in the distance. Flamenco can be fiery, but at night it can also be the most tender music in the world. That is the side of it you will hear here.
