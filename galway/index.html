The place

Galway by night

Galway sits on the wild west coast of Ireland, where the River Corrib runs into Galway Bay and the Atlantic begins. It is a small, colourful city of stone harbours, narrow streets and music drifting out of open doors.

To the west lies Connemara: a wide, quiet land of bogs, lakes, stone walls and the mountains called the Twelve Bens, often wrapped in soft mist. Out in the bay, the Aran Islands rise from the sea, criss-crossed with old dry-stone walls.

Evenings here belong to music. In pubs across the city, musicians sit around a table and play the old tunes together, one leading, the others following, for no reason other than the joy of it.

The harp is the national symbol of Ireland: you find it on its coins and its passports. Centuries ago, travelling harpers such as Turlough O'Carolan went from house to house, playing for their hosts. The music on this channel brings that harp together with the tin whistle, low strings and a soft bodhrán drum, slow and calm, like mist moving over the hills.
