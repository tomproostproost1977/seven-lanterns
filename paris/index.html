The place

Paris by night

Paris lies on both banks of the Seine, a river crossed by more than thirty bridges. By day it is busy and grand. By night, when the lamps come on along the quays, it becomes a city of small lit windows and slow walks.

On the hill of Montmartre, above the rooftops, painters once rented cheap studios and met in its cafés. Its steep stairways and narrow streets still lead up to the white domes of the Sacré-Cœur, where the whole city spreads out below.

The café is the living room of Paris: a small round table, a coffee or a glass of wine, a book, and nobody in a hurry. Along the river, green wooden boxes of second-hand books line the stone walls, as they have for more than a century.

Jazz arrived in Paris in the 1920s and never left. In the 1930s the guitarist Django Reinhardt and the violinist Stéphane Grappelli gave it a French accent, and the accordion of the old dance halls joined in. The music on this channel is the quiet, late-evening side of that tradition: soft piano, brushed drums, a walking bass, and a touch of accordion.
