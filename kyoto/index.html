The place

Kyoto by night

Kyoto lies in a valley ringed by wooded hills in the heart of Japan. For more than a thousand years, from 794 until 1868, it was the home of the emperor and the capital of the country.

Some two thousand temples and shrines are scattered through the city. Behind their wooden gates you find raked gravel gardens, moss that has grown undisturbed for centuries, and ponds where koi drift slowly under maple leaves.

In the west of the city, paths wind through the tall bamboo forest of Arashiyama, where the stems creak softly in the wind. In the old streets of Gion, paper lanterns glow outside wooden teahouses, and on rainy evenings the stone lanes shine like water.

The music on this channel is built from two old Japanese voices. The koto is a long zither with thirteen strings, plucked so that each note rings and fades. The shakuhachi is a bamboo flute once played by wandering monks as a form of meditation: one breath, one sound. Listen to how much space they leave between the notes, and let your breathing fill it.
