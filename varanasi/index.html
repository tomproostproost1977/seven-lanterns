The place

Varanasi by night

Varanasi lies on the banks of the river Ganges in northern India. It is one of the oldest living cities in the world and the holiest city of Hinduism, also known as Kashi, the City of Light.

Along the river, more than eighty ghats, long flights of stone steps, lead down to the water. At dawn, people come to bathe, pray and greet the sun, while boats drift slowly through the morning mist.

Every evening, as darkness falls, priests on the ghats perform the aarti: they raise great brass lamps of fire to the river, while bells ring and conch shells sound. Visitors set small leaf boats afloat, each carrying flowers and a tiny flame, and hundreds of lights drift away down the dark water.

Varanasi has a long tradition of classical music; the sitar master Ravi Shankar was born here. The music on this channel follows the slow opening of a raga: the bansuri, a simple bamboo flute, and the sitar, over the endless hum of the tanpura and a soft tabla. There is no hurry in it. Each note is allowed to unfold, the way the river moves.
