The place

Shiraz by night

Shiraz lies in a high valley in southern Iran, surrounded by mountains. For centuries it has been called the city of poets, gardens and nightingales.

Two of the most loved Persian poets are buried here. Hafez rests in a garden of orange trees and pools, and people still visit his tomb in the evening to read his verses aloud. Saadi lies a little further out of town, in a garden of his own.

The city is known for its gardens: long courtyards with water channels, cypress trees and roses. In spring the scent of orange blossom fills the streets. In the old town, the Nasir al-Mulk mosque fills with coloured light each morning, when the sun shines through its stained-glass windows.

The music on this channel borrows from that world: the shimmering santur, the breathy reed flute called the ney, and the bowed kamancheh, played slowly over a warm, distant pulse. You do not need to know anything about Persian music to enjoy it. Just let it carry you into a quiet garden at night.
