About

One lantern, one city, one sound

Seven Lanterns is a small independent music label based in Spain, making long, slow music for the quiet hours.

In the old cities of the world, the night was lit by lanterns: hung in gateways and courtyards, carried through narrow streets, set beside still water. Each one made a small, warm circle of light in the dark. That is the feeling we try to make with sound.

Every lantern belongs to one city and one musical tradition, and we never mix them. Lantern of Shiraz plays Persian ambient with santur, ney and kamancheh. Lantern of Yerevan follows the Armenian duduk. Lantern of Seville plays flamenco guitar the way it sounds after midnight. Lantern of Kyoto brings Japanese zen with koto and shakuhachi, Lantern of Paris the soft café jazz of a rainy evening, Lantern of Galway the Celtic harp and tin whistle, and Lantern of Varanasi the slow Indian raga of bansuri, sitar and tanpura.

Each lantern releases one-hour mixes for sleep, focus and quiet evenings, and a free sleep guide that turns the music into a gentle evening ritual.

How the music is made

Honest about our tools

Our tracks are created with AI music tools. We write the musical direction for every lantern, generate many versions, choose the few that feel right, then arrange, sequence, crossfade and master them into one-hour mixes. Every video is labelled as AI-assisted on YouTube, and we will always say so.

We think that is the fair way to do it. It lets a very small label make a lot of calm music. It also means our music is an interpretation inspired by these traditions, not a recording of their masters.

If a lantern moves you, go and find the real thing: Djivan Gasparyan on the duduk, Hossein Alizadeh and Kayhan Kalhor for Persian classical music, Paco de Lucía and Vicente Amigo for flamenco guitar, Goro Yamaguchi on the shakuhachi, Django Reinhardt and Stéphane Grappelli for Paris jazz, Derek Bell on the Irish harp, Hariprasad Chaurasia on the bansuri and Ravi Shankar on the sitar. Their recordings are the source of the light.
